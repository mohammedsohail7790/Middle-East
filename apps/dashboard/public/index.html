Troubleshooting & Edge Cases

What if the AI accidentally books a duplicate appointment? +
The AI checks your calendar via integration before booking. If a time slot is already taken, it will not book it. Duplicates are extremely rare. If one occurs, you can cancel it manually, and we will credit you 10 minutes of overage.
Can the AI handle angry or aggressive callers? +
Yes. The AI is trained to remain calm, polite, and de-escalate. If a caller becomes abusive, the AI will say:
"I'm sorry you're upset. I'm an AI, so I can't get angry. I'll take a message and have a human call you back within one hour. Is that okay?"
If the caller continues to yell, the AI will politely end the call and send you an urgent email alert.
Do you support call recording? +
Yes, but only with caller consent. The AI will say: "This call may be recorded for quality and training purposes." If the caller says no, the AI does not record. Recordings are stored for 30 days.
What happens if my internet goes down? +
Call IQ runs entirely in the cloud. Your internet does not matter. Calls route through our servers. As long as your phone number is forwarded to us or ported to us, calls will still be answered even if your office loses power or internet.

States with AI Disclosure Laws (Enacted or Pending)

As of 2025, several states have passed or introduced legislation requiring businesses to disclose when consumers are interacting with AI.

State Status Key Requirements Penalties

Massachusetts Pending (SB 243) Disclosure required regardless of whether consumer is misled or damaged UDAP violation
⚠️ Important Note on Extraterritorial Reach: Even if your business is not located in a state with an AI disclosure law, you may still need to comply if your customers are located in that state. For example: A Texas law firm using an AI receptionist must comply with Utah's disclosure law if a potential client from Salt Lake City calls. This is why we recommend keeping AI disclosure enabled. It is the simplest way to stay compliant across all 50 states.

One-Party Consent vs. All-Party Consent

In the United States, call recording laws fall into two categories:

Type Definition Number of States
One-Party Consent Only one person in the conversation needs to know the call is being recorded 38 states + DC
All-Party Consent Every person in the conversation must consent to the recording 12 states

Penalties for Violating Call Recording Laws

Violation Potential Penalties
Criminal Fines up to $10,000, imprisonment (varies by state), felony charges in some states (FL, MD)
Civil Damages, attorney fees, statutory penalties ($1,000–$5,000 per violation)
Federal Wiretap Act violations: $10,000 fine, up to 5 years imprisonment
📞 If You Record Calls Elsewhere: Even if your business is in a one-party consent state, recording a call with a person in an all-party consent state requires that person's consent. The stricter law often applies.
