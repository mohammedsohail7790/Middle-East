HVAC Terminology for Your AI Database
Here is a breakdown of essential HVAC terms, organized by category to help the AI understand context.

1. System Types & Components
These are the core physical parts and types of systems the AI will hear about most often.

Air Conditioner: A system that cools and dehumidifies indoor air .

Furnace: An indoor unit that heats air by burning fuel (gas, oil) or using electricity and distributes it through the house .

Heat Pump: A versatile system that can both heat and cool a home by transferring heat instead of generating it .

Boiler: A closed vessel that heats water or steam for radiant heat, often through radiators or baseboards .

Air Handler: The indoor unit that contains the blower, evaporator coil, and filter to circulate conditioned air .

Compressor: Often called the "heart" of the AC system, it's located in the outdoor unit and circulates refrigerant .

Evaporator Coil: The indoor coil that absorbs heat from the air .

Condenser Coil: The outdoor coil that releases absorbed heat to the outside air .

Ductwork / Ducts: The network of pipes or passages that distribute air throughout a building .

Mini-Split / Ductless System: A system where an outdoor unit connects to one or more indoor air handlers without using ducts .

Package Unit: A single unit that contains both heating and cooling components, usually installed on a rooftop or a concrete slab .

Thermostat: The control system that monitors temperature and signals the HVAC equipment to turn on or off .

2. Efficiency Ratings & Measurements
These terms are critical for understanding conversations about energy costs, equipment performance, and upgrades.

SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio): A rating of an air conditioner's or heat pump's cooling efficiency. Higher SEER means higher efficiency .

AFUE (Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency): A measure of how efficiently a furnace converts fuel into heat over a typical year, expressed as a percentage .

HSPF (Heating Seasonal Performance Factor): A measure of a heat pump's heating efficiency. Higher numbers mean better efficiency .

EER (Energy Efficiency Ratio): A rating of an air conditioner's efficiency at a specific, fixed outdoor temperature .

BTU (British Thermal Unit): The standard unit of measurement for heating and cooling capacity. It's the energy needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit .

Tonnage: A unit of measurement for cooling capacity. One ton of cooling equals 12,000 BTUs per hour .

CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute): A measurement of the volume of air flowing through the system .

3. Common Service, Repair, & Diagnostic Terms
This vocabulary is essential for identifying problems, scheduling the right service, and understanding technician feedback.

Refrigerant: The chemical fluid in an AC or heat pump that absorbs and releases heat to cool the air .

R-22 / Freon: An older type of refrigerant being phased out .

R-410A / Puron: A newer, more environmentally friendly refrigerant that has replaced R-22 .

Heat Exchanger: A critical furnace component that transfers heat to the air. A crack in it is a serious safety issue .

Load Calculation: A mathematical assessment to determine the correct heating and cooling capacity needed for a home .

Short Cycling: When an HVAC system turns on and off frequently without completing a full cycle, indicating a problem .

Capacitor: An electrical component that stores energy and provides a starting boost to motors like the compressor and blower fan. A common point of failure .

Zoning System / Dampers: A system that divides a home into separate areas, each controlled by its own thermostat and dampers in the ductwork .

Ignitor / Pilot Light: The component that ignites the gas burner in a furnace. Older systems use a standing pilot light, while newer ones use an electronic ignitor .

Flame Sensor: A safety device that detects if a burner has properly ignited .

Inducer Motor / Draft Inducer: A fan that pulls combustion gases through the heat exchanger and out the flue .

Carbon Monoxide (CO): A dangerous, odorless gas produced by incomplete combustion that furnace issues can cause .

4. Adjectives & Problem Descriptors
These words help the AI gauge the urgency and nature of the call.

No Heat / No Cool / Not Working: Indicates a complete system failure .

Strange Noises (e.g., grinding, banging, clicking): Descriptors that can help identify specific mechanical failures .

Leaking / Refrigerant Leak: Loss of cooling capacity due to refrigerant escaping .

Freezing Up / Ice on Unit: Ice formation on the unit or lines, often due to airflow or refrigerant issues .

Tune-Up / Maintenance: A routine service visit for inspection and cleaning .

Not Keeping Up / Not Reaching Temperature: Indicates the system is running but unable to meet the thermostat setting.

💡 How to Use This List
To get the most out of this vocabulary, you can guide your engineer on how to implement it:

Categorization: Group terms as shown above. This helps the AI understand the context (e.g., a "compressor" is a part, "short cycling" is a problem).

Synonym Mapping: Connect common phrases to technical terms. For example, if a caller says "my Freon is low," the AI should connect that to the terms refrigerant or leak.

Emergency Keywords: Flag terms like "carbon monoxide," "gas smell," "no heat" (in winter), and "electrical issue" to trigger priority routing or emergency protocols.

Clarifying Questions: Use this vocabulary to generate intelligent follow-up questions. If a caller says "my AC isn't working," the AI could ask, "Is the fan running, or is the unit completely silent?" or "Is there any ice forming on the lines?"



Plumbing Terminology for Your AI Database
This list is compiled from industry glossaries and technical resources. Organizing terms by category will help the AI discern context—for example, distinguishing between a "T&P valve" (a safety device) and a "mixing valve" (a temperature control).

1. System Types & Key Components
These are the core physical parts and systems the AI will hear about most often.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
DWV (Drain-Waste-Vent)	The system of pipes that removes wastewater and vents sewer gases. A foundational system concept.
Main Line / Water Main	The primary pipeline supplying water from the utility company or the main drain line receiving waste from all branches.
Septic Tank	An underground tank where solid waste is decomposed by bacteria, used where no sewer connection exists.
Sump Pump	A pump installed in a pit (sump) to remove accumulated water from basements or crawl spaces to prevent flooding.
Water Heater	An appliance for heating water. Can be tank-type (storage) or tankless/instantaneous.
Boiler	A sealed tank where water is heated to generate hot water or steam for heating systems.
Hydronic Heating	A heating system that circulates hot water through pipes to radiators, baseboards, or radiant floor systems.
2. Pipes, Materials & Fittings
These terms are critical for understanding conversations about installations, repairs, and material types.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)	Rigid black plastic pipe used for DWV systems.
PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)	White or gray rigid plastic pipe used for DWV and cold water systems.
CPVC (Chlorinated Polyvinyl Chloride)	Off-white or cream-colored plastic pipe rated for hot and cold water supply lines.
PEX (Cross-Linked Polyethylene)	Flexible, color-coded plastic tubing (red for hot, blue for cold) increasingly used for water supply lines.
Copper	A metal pipe and fitting material used for water supply systems.
Brass	An alloy (copper and zinc) used for faucets, valves, and fittings; also a slang term for faucets/fixtures.
Polybutylene (PB)	A gray or white flexible plastic tubing used for water supply in the late 20th century, now known to be prone to failure.
Fitting	A generic term for a piece (e.g., elbow, tee, coupling) that connects sections of pipe.
Nipple	A short, straight length of pipe with threads on both ends, used to connect fittings.
Elbow / Ell	A curved fitting, typically 90° or 45°, used to change pipe direction.
Tee	A T-shaped fitting used to create a branch connection where three pipes meet.
Wye Fitting	A Y-shaped fitting that connects two sections of pipe at a 45-degree angle for smoother flow.
Bushing	A fitting with internal and external threads, used to connect pipes of different sizes.
Dielectric Union	A special fitting that joins dissimilar metals (like copper and steel) to prevent galvanic corrosion.
No-Hub Coupling	A rubber sleeve with a stainless steel band used to join hubless cast-iron pipe.
3. Fixtures & Appliances
These are the endpoint devices where water is used or waste is collected. Distinguishing between a "fitting" and a "fixture" is important for accuracy.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Faucet	A fitting for controlling water flow into a basin; also called a "tap".
Fixture	A device that receives water and/or waste (e.g., toilet, sink, bathtub). Note: Often confused with "fitting" in casual speech.
Toilet / Water Closet	A fixture for receiving and flushing human waste. Key parts include the bowl (round or elongated), tank, and trapway.
Lavatory	Industry term for a bathroom sink or washbowl.
Sink	A fixture for washing, typically in a kitchen or utility room.
Bathtub	A large fixture for bathing. Features include an apron (front skirting) and end drain (left or right-hand orientation).
Shower	An enclosure for bathing. Components include the curb (raised edge), body sprays, and showerhead.
Bidet	A floor-mounted plumbing fixture for personal hygiene, similar in appearance to a toilet.
Urinal	A wall-hung or floor-mounted fixture for use by males, primarily in commercial settings.
4. Valves & Controls
These terms describe devices that start, stop, or regulate flow. This is a critical category for troubleshooting.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Shutoff Valve / Angle Stop	A valve used to stop water flow to a specific fixture (like a sink or toilet) for repairs. An angle stop has a 90-degree inlet/outlet configuration.
Ball Valve	A quarter-turn valve using a rotating ball with a hole to control flow; known for reliability and full shut-off.
Gate Valve	A valve that uses a sliding gate to control flow; often used as a main shut-off but less reliable than a ball valve. An OS&Y valve has a visible rising stem.
Check Valve	A one-way valve that allows flow in only one direction to prevent backflow.
Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV)	A valve that reduces high incoming water pressure to a safe, consistent level for a building.
Mixing Valve	A valve that blends hot and cold water to achieve a desired outlet temperature, enhancing safety and comfort.
Anti-Scald Device / Scald Guard	A valve or mechanism that prevents rapid temperature fluctuations to protect against scalding.
Temperature and Pressure (T&P) Relief Valve	A critical safety valve on water heaters that opens if temperature or pressure exceeds safe limits.
Diverter Valve	A valve that redirects water flow, e.g., from a tub spout to a showerhead, or to a kitchen sink sprayer.
Ballcock / Fill Valve	The valve assembly in a toilet tank, controlled by a float, that refills the tank after a flush.
5. Common Service, Repair, & Problem Descriptors
This vocabulary is essential for the AI to understand caller issues and prioritize emergencies.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Leak	An unintended escape of water from a pipe, fitting, or fixture.
Clog / Blockage	An obstruction in a drain, waste pipe, or toilet that prevents proper flow.
Drain Cleaning Tools	Tools used to clear clogs, such as an auger or snake (flexible cable), plunger, and blowbag.
Trap / P-Trap	The curved pipe (often U or J-shaped) under a sink or fixture that holds water to create a trap seal, preventing sewer gases from entering the building.
Cleanout	A fitting with a removable plug that provides access to the drain line for cleaning and inspection.
Backflow	The unwanted reverse flow of water, which can contaminate the potable water supply. Prevented by backflow preventers.
Water Hammer	A loud banging noise in pipes caused by a sudden stop in water flow (e.g., when a valve closes quickly). Can be fixed with a water hammer arrestor.
Rough-In	The phase of installation where pipes, drains, and vent lines are installed inside walls, floors, and ceilings before finishes are applied.
Sewer Gas	Toxic and potentially explosive gas (containing methane, hydrogen sulfide) resulting from decomposing waste. An indication of a dry trap or venting issue.
Gallons per Minute (GPM)	A standard measure of water flow rate from a faucet or showerhead.
Gallons per Flush (GPF)	A measure of the water volume a toilet uses per flush. Modern toilets are typically 1.6 GPF or less. Dual-flush toilets offer a choice of full or partial flush.
Potable Water	Water that is safe for drinking and domestic use.
Greywater / Gray Water	Wastewater from sinks, showers, and laundry (excluding toilets), which may be reused for irrigation in some systems.
Lead-Free	A regulatory standard (≤0.25% lead content) for materials in contact with potable water.
Effluent	Liquid waste, partially treated, that flows from a septic tank into the drain/leach field.
Leach Field / Absorption Field	A network of perforated pipes in a gravel bed that disperses effluent from a septic tank into the soil.
6. Sealing & Joining Materials
Term	Definition / Context for AI
Flux	A chemical paste applied to clean metal surfaces and promote the flow of solder during soldering.
Teflon Tape / Thread Seal Tape	A white tape wrapped around pipe threads to create a watertight seal on threaded joints.
Plumber's Putty	A pliable, dough-like putty used to create a watertight seal on the underside of faucets and drains where they meet a sink or tub.
O-Ring	A round rubber gasket that compresses to create a seal, often in valve stems and slip-joint connections.
Wax Ring	A ring of wax used to create a watertight and airtight seal between the base of a toilet and the closet flange on the floor.
Gasket	A flat ring, often made of rubber or fiber, used to create a seal between two metal surfaces.
Dope / Pipe Joint Compound	A lubricant and sealant paste applied to pipe threads.

Electrical Terminology for Your AI Database
This list draws from authoritative sources including the National Electrical Code (NEC) definitions  and comprehensive electronics dictionaries , organized to help the AI understand context and route calls appropriately.

1. System Types & Core Electrical Concepts
These fundamental terms describe the basic principles and infrastructure of electrical systems.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
AC (Alternating Current)	Electric current that periodically reverses direction; standard for grid power distribution and mains power in homes .
DC (Direct Current)	Electric current flowing consistently in one direction; used in batteries, electronics, and solar power systems .
Voltage	Electrical pressure or force that pushes current through a conductor; measured in volts.
Current	The flow of electrons through a conductor; measured in amperes (amps).
Resistance	Opposition to the flow of current; measured in ohms.
Power	Rate of doing electrical work; calculated as voltage × current; measured in watts .
Service	The conductors and equipment delivering electricity from the utility to the building .
Feeder	Circuit conductors between the service equipment and the final branch-circuit overcurrent device .
Branch Circuit	Circuit conductors between the final overcurrent device and the outlets .
Grounding	Connecting electrical systems to earth for safety and equipment protection.
Bonding	Permanent joining of metal parts to ensure electrical conductivity and safe current-carrying capacity .
Single-Phase	Standard residential power delivery using two voltage phases 180 degrees apart.
Three-Phase	Commercial/industrial power using three alternating currents, providing smoother and more efficient power delivery.
2. Equipment & Components
These are the physical devices and hardware the AI will hear about most often.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Panelboard / Breaker Panel / Load Center	Central distribution point dividing electrical power into branch circuits, protected by circuit breakers .
Switchboard	Large, free-standing power distribution assembly with switches, overcurrent devices, and instruments .
Disconnect Switch	Device that opens and closes a circuit, manually operated, that can safely interrupt current .
Circuit Breaker	Automatic device that opens a circuit during overcurrent conditions without damaging itself .
Fuse	Overcurrent protection device that opens by melting a fusible element when current exceeds rated value .
AFCI (Arc-Fault Circuit Interrupter)	Breaker that detects dangerous electrical arcing and shuts down the circuit to prevent fires .
GFCI (Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupter)	Device that quickly shuts off power when detecting current leakage to ground, preventing shock.
Transformer	Device that transfers electrical energy between circuits through electromagnetic induction, changing voltage levels.
Conductor	Material (typically copper or aluminum wire) that carries electrical current .
Raceway	Enclosed channel designed expressly for holding wires, cables, or busbars (conduit, tubing, wireways) .
Conduit	Tube or trough for protecting electrical wiring (rigid metal, EMT, PVC, flexible) .
Busbar	Metallic strip or bar that conducts electricity within switchboards, panelboards, or busway systems.
Outlet	Point on wiring system where current is taken to supply equipment (receptacle, lighting outlet) .
Receptacle	Contact device installed at outlet for connection of an attachment plug .
Meter / Watt-Hour Meter	Device measuring and recording electrical energy consumption.
3. Wire Types, Sizing & Materials
These terms are essential for understanding specifications, materials, and installations.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
AWG (American Wire Gauge)	North American standard system for wire diameters; smaller gauge numbers = larger wire .
Ampacity	Maximum current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding temperature rating .
Insulated Conductor	Conductor encased within material recognized as electrical insulation .
Bare Conductor	Conductor without covering or insulation (e.g., service grounding wire) .
THHN / THWN	Common insulation types: Thermoplastic High Heat-resistant Nylon-coated / Thermoplastic Heat and Water-resistant Nylon-coated.
NM Cable (Romex)	Nonmetallic sheathed cable; common residential wiring with conductors inside plastic sheath.
MC Cable (Metal-Clad)	Flexible cable with metal armor for physical protection.
UF Cable (Underground Feeder)	Moisture-resistant cable rated for direct burial.
Conductor Size	Physical cross-sectional dimension determining current-carrying capacity.
Stranded vs. Solid	Multiple small strands twisted vs. single solid wire; stranded is more flexible.
4. Common Service, Repair, & Diagnostic Terms
This vocabulary helps identify problems, schedule appropriate service, and understand technician feedback.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Overcurrent	Excessive current relative to normal operating current; includes overloads, short circuits, and ground faults .
Short Circuit	Unintentional connection between ungrounded conductors of low impedance, causing excessive current.
Ground Fault	Unintentional connection between an ungrounded conductor and ground/equipment ground .
Arc Fault	Dangerous electrical discharge characterized by high heat, potentially causing fires .
Open Circuit	Broken or interrupted path preventing current flow.
Trip / Tripped Breaker	Automatic opening of circuit breaker due to overcurrent condition.
Flickering Lights	Common symptom indicating loose connections, voltage fluctuations, or failing components.
Dead Outlet	Outlet with no power.
Plenum	Compartment or chamber connected to air distribution system; requires special plenum-rated cable .
Concealed Wiring	Wiring installed within walls or partitions, inaccessible without damaging finish .
Working Space	Clear area around electrical equipment for safe operation and maintenance (minimum 3 feet in front) .
Load Calculation	Determining total electrical demand to size service and equipment properly.
Voltage Drop	Reduction in voltage along a conductor due to resistance; excessive drop causes equipment issues.
5. Safety & Code Terminology
These terms are critical for understanding regulatory compliance, inspections, and safety protocols.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
NEC (National Electrical Code)	The standard for safe electrical installation in the US; often called "the book" .
AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction)	Organization or individual (typically electrical inspector) enforcing code requirements .
Approved	Acceptable to the authority having jurisdiction .
Listed / Labeled	Equipment included in a list published by an approved testing laboratory (UL, ETL, CSA) meeting appropriate standards.
Qualified Person	One familiar with construction/operation of equipment and hazards involved (licensed electrician, professional engineer) .
Service Entrance	Point where power enters building, including service drop/mast, meter, and main disconnect.
Main Bonding Jumper	Connection between grounded circuit conductor and equipment grounding conductor at service.
Grounding Electrode	Device establishing direct connection to earth (ground rod, water pipe, concrete-encased electrode).
Permit	Official authorization required before beginning electrical work.
Inspection	Official examination of work by AHJ for code compliance.
6. Residential vs. Commercial Contexts
These terms help distinguish between typical residential issues and more complex commercial/industrial work.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Residential Service	Typically 100-400 amp, single-phase, 120/240V.
Commercial Service	Typically 120/208V or 277/480V, three-phase, higher amperage.
Subpanel	Secondary panel fed from main service, serving a specific area or addition.
Main Lug Only (MLO)	Panel without main breaker; used as subpanel.
Main Breaker	Primary overcurrent device for entire panel.
Knob and Tube	Early wiring method (pre-1950s) with ceramic knobs and tubes; obsolete and often requiring replacement.
Aluminum Wiring	Wiring used in 1960s-70s requiring special connections due to different expansion properties and oxidation concerns.
Service Upgrade	Replacing older electrical service to increase capacity or meet modern safety standards.
7. Modern & Emerging Technologies
As electrical work increasingly intersects with smart home technology, these terms are becoming more common in service calls.

Term	Definition / Context for AI
Smart Panel / Load Center	Modern panel with integrated monitoring, remote control, and smart breaker capabilities.
EV Charger / EVSE (Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment)	Equipment for charging electric vehicles; installation is a growing electrical service call category.
Solar / PV (Photovoltaic) System	Solar electric generation system requiring interconnection with electrical service.
Battery Backup / Energy Storage System	Battery banks storing energy for backup or load shifting.
Generator / Transfer Switch	Backup power source and automatic/manual switch isolating from utility during outages .
Smart Thermostat	WiFi-enabled thermostat requiring C-wire (common wire) for continuous power.
Whole Home Surge Protector	Device installed at panel protecting entire home from external surges.
AFCI/GFCI Combination Breaker	Modern breaker providing both arc-fault and ground-fault protection.
8. Electrical Problems & Emergency Descriptors
These words help the AI gauge urgency and prioritize emergency calls.

Term / Phrase	Likely Meaning / Priority Level
"No power" / "Dead outlet"	Complete loss of power; could be tripped breaker or larger issue.
"Breaker keeps tripping"	Persistent overcurrent or ground fault; indicates underlying problem needing diagnosis.
"Spark / Sparks"	Visible arcing; potentially serious safety issue.
"Smell burning / burning smell"	Potential overheating or fire hazard; high priority emergency.
"Buzzing / humming noise"	Loose connection, failing breaker, or faulty device.
"Shock / tingling"	Ground fault or improper grounding; safety emergency.
"Lights dim when..."	Voltage drop under load; possible undersized circuit or loose connection.
"Partial power"	Only some circuits working; could indicate lost phase (emergency for commercial).
"Main breaker tripped"	Whole service interruption; could be serious overload or fault.
"Arcing / crackling"	Visible or audible electrical discharge; fire hazard emergency.
"Power outage"	No utility power; may be weather-related or neighborhood-wide.
"Outlet hot / warm"	Overheating at connection point; potential fire hazard.
